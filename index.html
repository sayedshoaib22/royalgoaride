Goa Travel Guide

Your Complete Goa Travel Guide

Everything you need to plan the perfect Goa trip — beaches, food, tips, and more.

Beaches

Best Beaches in Goa

North Goa: Baga, Calangute, Anjuna, Vagator, and Arambol offer lively atmospheres with beach shacks and water sports. South Goa: Palolem, Agonda, Colva, and Benaulim are quieter with pristine sands. Morjim is ideal for witnessing Olive Ridley turtle nesting.

Families

Family-Friendly Goa

Families love Palolem's calm waters, Goa Science Centre in Panjim, Bhagwan Mahaveer Wildlife Sanctuary, the spice plantations of Ponda, and Dudhsagar Falls. South Goa's beaches are generally safer and less crowded — perfect for children.

Couples

Goa for Couples

Couples should not miss Agonda's serene sunsets, the Sunset Point at Chapora Fort, the atmospheric lanes of Fontainhas in Panjim, candlelit dinners at Palolem beach shacks, and evening cruises on the Mandovi River. Best explored with your own rental car.

Hidden Gems

Hidden Attractions

Beyond the tourist trail: Butterfly Beach (accessible by boat or trek from Palolem), Divar Island (serene countryside by ferry), Cola Beach (hidden lagoon), Cabo de Rama Fort (dramatic cliff views), and the ancient Tambdi Surla Temple deep in the Western Ghats.

Food

Local Food & Drink

Must-try Goan dishes: Fish Curry Rice, Prawn Balchão, Chicken Xacuti, Beef Vindaloo, Goan Sausage, and Bebinca dessert. Wash it down with Feni (cashew or coconut spirit) — Goa's unique local drink. Best experienced at beachside shacks and local Goan restaurants away from tourist strips.

When to Visit

Best Time to Visit Goa

Peak Season (Nov–Feb): Perfect weather, 28–32°C, all beaches and attractions open. Busiest and most expensive. Shoulder Season (Sep–Oct & Mar–May): Less crowded, good deals on accommodation and car rentals. Monsoon (Jun–Aug): Lush green landscapes and very low prices, but some beaches and shacks close.

Driving Tips

Driving Tips in Goa

First time driving in Goa? Here's what you need to know before you hit the road.

Speed Limits

Speed limit is 40 km/h in cities and 60 km/h on state highways. Goa has regular speed checks — always carry your licence and rental documents.

Avoid Peak Hours

Tourist season (November–February) sees heavy traffic near popular beaches between 10am–12pm and 5pm–7pm. Plan your drives early morning for a smoother experience.

Monsoon Driving

Roads can get slippery during heavy rains (June–August). Drive slowly, use headlights, and avoid flooded roads. Some interior roads may be inaccessible.

Use Google Maps

Google Maps works very well in Goa. Download offline maps for your travel area before starting — some remote beach roads have poor mobile signal.

Parking at Beaches

Popular beaches like Baga and Calangute have paid parking areas. Arrive early to get spots close to the beach. Never park blocking traffic — local wardens are active.

Fuel Stations

Keep your tank topped up when exploring South Goa's remote areas or heading to Dudhsagar. Fuel stations can be sparse on certain routes. Check before you start.

Carry Documents

Always carry your original Driving Licence, rental agreement, and vehicle papers in the car. Police checkpoints are common, especially on NH66 and during festival seasons.

Emergency Contact

Save our WhatsApp number before you start driving. We're available 24/7 for any roadside assistance, navigation help, or emergency support during your rental period.
